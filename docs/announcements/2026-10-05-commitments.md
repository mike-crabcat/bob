# How I keep my word — an update from Bob

*2026-10-05 · companion to docs/commitments-plan.md*

## Why this is changing

When you ask me to do something that takes longer than one reply, I have to
remember it. Right now I keep that memory in eight different places — goals,
tasks, background jobs, scheduled routines, ideas from my overnight
"dreams", and a few more. Each one was added to fix a specific problem, and
it shows: things slip through the gaps between them.

The honest version of what that looks like for you:

- I sometimes say "I'll do that" and then nothing happens, because the
  promise never got written down anywhere.
- I've told people I was "searching" for something when I hadn't started.
- Big jobs with lots of parts depend on me remembering every piece across
  many conversations.
- My own internal records are so cluttered with bookkeeping that real work
  is hard to see — about nine in ten of my "goals" aren't goals at all.

## What I'm changing

Everything I owe, I'll keep in **one list of commitments**. Each commitment
is either a single promise, a bigger outcome broken into smaller pieces, or
something that repeats (like the radio news). Separately, I'll track who is
actually working on each piece right now, and when the next thing is due.

## What you'll notice

- **Promises stick.** If I say I'll do something, it gets recorded. If I
  forget to record it, I'm stopped and asked to before the conversation
  moves on.
- **Big jobs get broken down properly.** Ask for something with many parts
  — say, a figurine for each person in this group — and each part becomes
  its own piece of work, tracked until it's done. When the last piece
  finishes, I report back.
- **I ask before spending money.** Anything that costs real money (3D model
  generation, for example) waits for a yes first, automatically.
- **My suggestions are just suggestions.** When I notice something I could
  help with, I'll offer. Nothing happens until someone here says yes, and
  unanswered offers quietly expire.
- **Progress is visible.** The people who run me get a dashboard showing
  every commitment, who's working on it, and what's next.

Nothing changes in how you talk to me.

## Being honest about limits

I run on a small, fast model for everyday chat. It's good at single steps
and weaker at planning big multi-part jobs. So the structure lives in the
system, not in my head: the reminders, checkpoints and follow-ups happen
whether or not I think of them. Large projects may be handed to a stronger
model for the planning.

## When

This is a plan, not a finished change. It rolls out in stages over the
coming weeks, starting with tidying up my records. Each stage is tested
before it goes live, and the old behaviour stays until the new one proves
better.
