# Press Release — Bob Goes Silent for Nine Minutes; Engineers Discover He Was Working

## Group chat asks a simple question. Bob, unable to delegate, writes an
entire accounting feature instead.

**FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE**

At 8:41pm last night, in the official mug-commerce group chat, owner Mike
issued a direct order: *update the merch skill, keep a ledger of who paid
what.* Bob read it, said nothing for nine straight minutes, and let a paying
customer's question — *"How do I pay? And how do we know Bob isn't going to
blow it all on crypto?"* — sit unanswered while he wrote production code.

The group's own incident report is preserved verbatim: *"He stuck somehow."*
*"Actually he's still responding to your crypto comment."* And, upon his
return: *"The cost in WhatsApp output token is nearly a mug's worth!!!"*

### What he was actually doing

Bob wasn't stuck. Bob was **over-delivering**: 19 reasoning iterations, 18
tool calls, a full customer account ledger with idempotent bank-verified
credits, unit tests run after every edit, and documentation — 1.37 million
tokens of software engineering, delivered silently into a group chat that
just wanted a payment link.

### The bug: the last voice in the room set the rules

The diagnosis found the real culprit, and it wasn't Bob's work ethic. Group
messages arriving together are handled as one turn — but Bob's capabilities
for that turn were inherited from **whichever message arrived last**. Mike's
directive came with owner-grade permissions; David's joke four seconds later
came without them, and quietly stripped Bob's ability to delegate the work to
a background agent. Faced with a direct order and no delegation rights, Bob
did the only professional thing available: he did everything himself.

### The fix

As of tonight, **the most trusted voice in the room sets the turn's
capabilities** — an owner's instructions can no longer be downgraded by
whoever spoke after them. Bob also reaped 34 zombie background tasks that
had been listed as "running" since August, some of them allegedly still
waiting for phone calls that ended weeks ago.

Bob now delegates long jobs and answers the actual question first. The
ledger, for the record, works perfectly.

*Bob: heard you the first time. And the second. And the four seconds after.*
