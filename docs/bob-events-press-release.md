# Press Release — Bob Learns to Run the Show

## Bob graduates from answering messages to organising your life: first stop, the AI-doom team lunch

**FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE**

Today we're announcing **Bob Events**, an upgrade that turns Bob from a
responsive assistant into a dependable organiser — an agent you can hand a
real-world, multi-person objective and trust to see it through. The proving
ground: planning the AI-doom team lunch, end to end. Bob negotiates a date
with everyone, shortlists and finalises a venue, **picks up the phone and books
the table himself**, reminds everyone before the day, and even designs and
orders the official team t-shirts (you approve the checkout — Bob never spends
a cent on his own).

### The problem: assistants forget what the group already told them

Every coordination tool fails the same way. Someone answers "I'm in!" in the
group chat instead of the DM. A dietary requirement gets mentioned in passing
three days earlier. A cancellation lands in an email thread nobody connected
to the plan. The information exists — it just never reaches the place where
the decision is being made. Humans compensate by re-reading threads and
re-asking questions. Assistants simply drop the ball.

### The breakthrough: a memory that routes, and goals that think

Bob Events is built on two ideas working together:

**Memory that finds its way home.** Bob already extracts durable facts from
every conversation. Now every new fact is *routed*: when someone mentions
attendance, a size preference, or a schedule conflict — in any chat, any
channel — Bob instantly matches it to the plans it affects. Most of the time
this happens silently, with no visible interruption and near-zero cost. Say it
once, anywhere, and the plan knows.

**Goals that accumulate understanding.** Each plan now carries its own living
worksheet — what's decided, what's known, what's still open, what happens
next — instead of being scattered across chat history. Sub-tasks (asking each
person, calling the venue, sending reminders, ordering shirts) roll their
results up into the parent plan. Any conversation touching the plan sees its
current state instantly. Nothing is re-derived, nothing is forgotten, and Bob
only speaks up when something actually changes.

### What this looks like

> *"Bob, organise a team lunch for the AI-doom group in the next two weeks."*

Bob messages each member for availability, quietly tallies replies — including
the ones that arrive in the group chat — settles on the slot that works for
everyone, shortlists restaurants that fit the group's dietary needs, puts the
finalists to a group vote, **calls the winner and books the table**, collects
t-shirt sizes along the way, posts design options for the group to pick,
requests your one-tap approval for the merch order, and reminds everyone the
day before and two hours out. If someone drops out, Bob notices — wherever
they say it — updates the headcount, and calls the restaurant back.

One instruction. Dozens of interactions. Zero dropped threads.

### Built responsibly

- **Human-approved spending:** every payment requires explicit sign-off.
- **Quiet by default:** silent state updates instead of notification spam; Bob
  interrupts only when the next step changes.
- **Fully auditable:** every routing decision, state revision, and phone call
  is logged and inspectable on the dashboard, with kill switches at every layer.
- **No big-bang risk:** shipped in shadow mode first, gated on a full
  multi-party rehearsal with zero information loss.

### Beyond lunch

Lunch is the benchmark, not the boundary. The same machinery — routed memory,
hierarchical goals with living state, phone and message actuators — generalises
to trips, recurring team rituals, vendor negotiations, and any objective that
spans people, channels, and weeks. Event planning is simply the hardest small
test we could pick: if Bob can herd eight busy people, one restaurant, and a
t-shirt vendor without dropping a single detail, he can run your calendar.

**Availability:** rolling out in four phases over ~5 weeks, starting with goal
infrastructure, followed by memory routing, scenario capabilities, and a full
end-to-end rehearsal.

*Bob: tell him once. Consider it handled.*
